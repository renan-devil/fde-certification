## Part 1. Read a plant P&L

---
<!-- 7 | Framework -->
## Revenue is volume times price times mix; every cost is variable or fixed

| Line | What to ask |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Volume trend first, then price, then mix. Mix shifts explain half of everything else. |
| Variable costs | Move with volume: materials, energy, consumables, freight, overtime premium. |
| Fixed costs | Do not move on the planning horizon: salaried labor, depreciation, most maintenance. |
| Contribution margin | Revenue minus variable costs. The answer to "what is one more unit worth?" |
| Plant EBIT | What the director is judged on. Corporate allocations sit on top: know them, do not fight them. |

---
<!-- 8 | Data -->
## Materials are 60% of Polymex's revenue, so scrap is a materials story before it is a quality story

- Resins, additives and pigments cost €89.2M on €148.0M of revenue.
- In process industries materials are typically 50 to 70% of cost.
- A 4.2% scrap rate on €89.2M of material input is €3.7M of resin thrown away each year, before counting the capacity that produced it.

Source: Polymex plant P&L and operational annex, last twelve months.

<!-- 11 | Framework -->
## Read any plant P&L in twenty minutes, in five moves

1. **Revenue.** Volume trend and mix shift first.
2. **Gross margin.** Against last year and against the sector.
3. **Operational annex.** OEE, scrap, OTIF, inventory days, before any more cost lines.
4. **Anomalies.** Any line moving faster than volume.
5. **Three hypotheses.** Written before anyone explains the plant to you.

---
<!-- 12 | Detail -->
## Speak the director's vocabulary without notes by Friday

| Term | Meaning, and the question to ask |
|---|---|
| OTIF | On time, in full. Against the promise date or the request date? |
| Demonstrated capacity | What the plant has actually produced at its best sustained rate, not the nameplate |
| Takt | Available time divided by customer demand: the pace the plant must hold |
| MTBF and MTTR | Mean time between failures, mean time to repair |
| First-pass yield | Good the first time, without rework. Rework hides between first-pass and final yield |
| DIO | Days of inventory outstanding, against sector peers |
| Frozen horizon | The days of the schedule nobody is allowed to change |

## Part 2. The bottleneck and the demand fork

---
<!-- 15 | Hero -->
## An hour lost at the bottleneck is lost for the whole plant. An hour saved anywhere else is a mirage.

---
<!-- 16 | Framework -->
## The constraint sets the plant's output, and at Polymex the constraint is extrusion

| Stage | Daily minutes, average over Q1 2026 |
|---|---|
| Mixing | Starved 60, blocked 1,169: waiting for extrusion to take its batches |
| Extrusion | Starved 34, blocked 25: always busy, never waiting |
| Packing | Starved 1,658, blocked 35: waiting for extrusion to feed it |

Upstream blocked, downstream starved, the constraint busy: the textbook signature. Every improvement this week targets extrusion.

Source: Polymex stage daily file, Q1 2026 (Day 1 lab).

---
<!-- 17 | Framework -->
## What a freed hour is worth forks on one question: is the plant sold out?

| Situation | A freed hour at the constraint is worth |
|---|---|
| Sold out | The contribution margin of the extra tonnes, once sales confirms it can sell them |
| Not sold out | Only the variable cost it avoids: overtime, weekend shifts, outsourcing |
| Not sold out, but lead time loses orders | The contribution of the orders that shorter lead times win, if sales can name them |

Ask "what is your order book against demonstrated capacity?" in the first hour of every engagement.

---
<!-- 18 | Data -->
## Polymex is not sold out, but two accounts would take 6% more volume if lead times improved

- Order book: 91% of demonstrated capacity.
- Sales: two retail accounts would take +6% volume at current prices with shorter lead times.
- So a freed hour is worth avoided overtime today, and up to the contribution of 5,160 t a year if the lead time improves.

Source: Polymex operational annex; sales director, interview notes.

<!-- 19 | Data -->
## One point of extrusion availability is worth about 1,143 tonnes of good output a year

- Planned time: 7 lines × 3 shifts × 8 hours × 365 days = 61,320 hours a year.
- One point is 613 hours, at 2.30 t/h ideal, 86.3% performance and 93.9% quality.
- That is 1,143 good tonnes, worth €0.5M a year only if the plant can sell them.

Source: Polymex shift logs Q1 2026; Day 1 OEE definitions.

<!-- 20 | Framework -->
## Plants leak money through five families, and the last one is usually a symptom

- **Scrap and quality.** Material, plus the capacity that produced it. First-pass yield beats final yield.
- **Changeovers.** Capacity and start-up scrap. SMED discipline often removes 30 to 50% of changeover time.
- **Unplanned downtime.** The logged number is a floor; micro-stops hide below it.
- **Inventory.** Cash released once, carrying cost of 15 to 25% a year saved every year.
- **Expedites and overtime.** Almost always the visible symptom of the other four plus planning.

---
<!-- 21 | Detail -->
## Count every euro once: overtime and expedites are symptoms, not a sixth leak

- Polymex pays €0.9M of overtime premium, concentrated on Fridays and month-end.
- Those hours buy back capacity lost to changeovers and late plans.
- Size them separately and you count the same tonne twice.
- Name the double count out loud in your memo. A controller will look for it first.

## Part 3. Size in euros

---
<!-- 23 | Framework -->
## Size a leak in five moves, always in the same order

1. **Physical effect.** Hours, tonnes or kilos, with a source.
2. **Valuation path.** Which branch of the fork, which P&L line.
3. **Assumptions table.** Every number you did not measure.
4. **Low, base, high.** The driving assumption named in each case.
5. **Triangulate.** A second, independent path to the same number.

---
<!-- 24 | Detail -->
## The assumptions table is the deliverable; the point estimate is a by-product

| Assumption | Base | Range | Source | Who confirms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contribution per tonne | €447 | €400 to €480 | P&L, controller | Mireille's controller |
| Sellable extra volume | 5,160 t a year | 0 to 5,160 t | Sales interview | Sales director, in writing |
| SMED reduction of changeover time | 40% | 30 to 50% | Benchmark | Maintenance and Marc |
| Share of overtime avoidable | 30% | 0 to 50% | Overtime by day of week | HR and Hervé |

---
<!-- 25 | Framework -->
## Inventory releases cash once and saves carrying cost every year: never add the two

| Effect | Example: cut €10M of inventory |
|---|---|
| Cash released | €10M, once, in the year the stock goes down |
| Carrying cost saved | About €1.8M a year at 18%: capital, space, obsolescence, shrink |
| What you must never write | "€11.8M of savings" |

---
<!-- 26 | Detail -->
## Triangulate every big number, and treat a disagreement as a finding

- Two independent paths within 20 to 30% of each other: present with confidence.
- Further apart: you have found an error or a hidden assumption.
- Polymex example: the annex reports changeovers at 11.5% of scheduled hours; the extrusion logs say 8.8% for the quarter and 10.3% in March.
- Same plant, different perimeter and period. Ask the controller which one finance trusts before you price anything on it.

Source: Polymex operational annex; extrusion shift logs, Q1 2026.

---
<!-- 27 | Framework -->
## A problem is worth solving when it is big, measurable, controllable, owned and urgent

- **Big.** Worth at least ten times what it costs to fix.
- **Measurable.** The controller can compute the gain without you.
- **Controllable.** The plant holds the lever; corporate pricing does not count.
- **Owned.** A named person will change how they work.
- **Urgent.** Someone can say why now, in one sentence.

---
<!-- 28 | Framework -->
## One plant becomes an account in four steps: land, prove, replicate, expand

1. **Land.** One site, one problem, one sponsor.
2. **Prove.** A gain the client's controller signs.
3. **Replicate.** The same ontology and pipeline at a sister site, in half the time.
4. **Expand.** The next problem, on data you already cleaned.

---
<!-- 29 | Detail -->
## Choose the first problem for what it unlocks, not only for its size

- A gain finance can sign within ninety days beats a bigger one it cannot measure.
- Prefer the problem whose data foundation the next three use cases reuse.
- Prefer a problem that turns a user into a champion, not one that threatens her job.
- Stay clear of a political minefield in the first month, even if it is the biggest leak.

---
<!-- 30 | Detail -->
## Map the account before you map the plant

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Who owns the budget, site or group? | Decides who signs the next phase |
| What else is in flight: lean program, APS selection, ERP upgrade? | Your gain may already be claimed by someone else |
| How many sister sites run the same process? | The size of the prize if you replicate |
| Who has tried before, and what happened? | The scar tissue you will inherit |
| What does the director's boss measure him on? | His real "why now" |

---
<!-- 33 | Detail -->
## The register: one row per leak, nine columns, nothing hidden

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Leak and family | M07 micro-stops, unplanned downtime |
| Evidence | The number, its source and period |
| Physical effect | Tonnes or hours a year, annualized and labeled as such |
| Valuation path | Which branch of the fork, which P&L line |
| Low, base, high | In € a year, with the driving assumption |
| Recurring or one-time | Stated separately, never summed |
| Overlaps | Which other row counts the same tonne |
| Confidence | High, medium, low, and why |
| First question on site | To whom, and what answer would change the number |

## Three things to keep from this morning

1. Value a freed hour with the fork: sold out, not sold out, or lead time that wins named orders.
2. One more tonne is worth its contribution margin, never its price or its full cost.
3. The assumptions table, a range and a second path are what make a number credible.

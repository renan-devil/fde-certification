## Both cases are real and disguised: keep them that way

1. Names, sites, people and products are invented.
2. Financial figures are scaled and rounded; dates are shifted.
3. If you think you recognize a company, keep it to yourself.
4. Add nothing that is not in the case pack.
5. Gains are framed as throughput: the same team producing more.

---
<!-- 5 | Framework -->
## Run the whole week in two hours

1. **Diagnostic.** Problem statement, tree, priors.
2. **Sizing.** Assumptions, low and high, a second path.
3. **Validation.** A baseline the controller computes.
4. **Plan.** Ninety days, gates, kill criteria.
5. **Readout.** Five slides, answer first.

---
<!-- 6 | Framework -->
## Two plants, two different problems hiding behind the obvious one

| Case | What the sponsor asks | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| A. The compounder | "Where do we leave throughput, and what in ninety days?" | What nobody measures, and what the controller will need |
| B. The sock manufacturer | "Where do we start, and what is it worth?" | When defects are found, not only how many |

<!-- 7 | Detail -->
## The steering committee will ask you five questions

1. What is your baseline, and who computes it?
2. Which number is a run-rate, and which is realized?
3. What would make you stop at week four?
4. What do you not know yet, and how will you find out?
5. What do you need from us on Monday?

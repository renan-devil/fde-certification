## Objectives

By noon every participant can:

1. Read a plant P&L in twenty minutes and separate variable from fixed costs.
2. State what one more tonne is worth at Polymex (€447 of contribution) and why that number depends on demand.
3. Value freed capacity with the demand fork, and refuse to price it at full cost.
4. Size a leak with an assumptions table, a low and a high case, and a second path.
5. Explain why the first problem of an account is chosen for what it unlocks.

## Slide notes

### Opening (slides 1 to 5)

**Slide 2.** Let the line sit. Then: "Yesterday you found M07 losing 376 tonnes. Is that a lot? Nobody in this room can answer yet. By noon you will."

**Slide 5.** Read one row aloud and ask the room what it is worth. Collect three guesses on the whiteboard. You will come back to them at 11:40.

### Read a plant P&L (slides 6 to 12)

**Slide 7.** Make the room classify three lines live: energy (mostly variable), maintenance (mostly fixed, with a variable share), direct labor (fixed, except the overtime premium). The point is not precision; it is the reflex of asking.

**Slide 8.** Materials at 60% of revenue changes how you see scrap: a scrap point at Polymex is about €0.9M of resin before any capacity effect.

**Slide 9.** The most important number of the morning. Write €447 on the whiteboard next to the fork. Ask: "Hervé says a tonne is worth €1,721. Why is he wrong?" Then: "The controller's full cost is €1,608. Why must you never value a freed hour with it?" Because fixed costs do not move when you free an hour.

**Slide 10.** Do not read the lines. Point at three: overtime premium (a symptom), scrap disposal inside consumables (the visible tip of scrap), and allocated SG&A (politics, not operations).

**Slide 11.** Insist on move 5. People who explain a plant to you anchor you on their theory. Hervé's theory is planning.

**Slide 12.** Ask one participant to define OTIF. Then ask: "Against what date?" Most will not know that OTIF on promise date and on request date can differ by ten points.

### Exercise 1, model answer

Three strong hypotheses, each with its number and its first question:

| Hypothesis | The number | First question for the controller |
|---|---|---|
| Scrap is the biggest recurring leak | 4.2% of €89.2M of material input, plus €0.15M disposal | Is regrind recovered, and is the 4.2% net or gross of it? |
| Changeovers are eating the capacity buffer and the OTIF | 11.5% of scheduled hours, count doubled since January; OTIF 87% and slipping; overtime on Fridays | How is changeover time measured, and since when? |
| Inventory is too high for the sector | DIO 78 days against about 55 for peers | Which families hold the excess, and is any of it obsolete? |

Acceptable alternatives: energy intensity (ask for kWh per tonne by line), maintenance (ask for the planned against reactive split). Weak answers: "costs are too high", "labor is expensive". Push them to a number.

### Bottleneck and demand fork (slides 14 to 21)

**Slide 15.** One field story: a team spent six weeks speeding up a packing line that was starved half the day. Zero tonnes gained. The lesson: find the constraint before you touch anything.

**Slide 16.** The participants found this yesterday. Make them say it, not you.

**Slide 17.** Draw it on the whiteboard as a fork with three branches. This is the single concept the exam tests most in this module.

**Slide 18.** The nuance that separates strong teams: Polymex is not sold out, so freed hours are worth little directly, but the lead-time unlock is real and capped at 5,160 t a year (6% of 86,000 t). Ask: "Who must confirm the 6% in writing?" The sales director, with named accounts.

**Slide 19.** Walk the arithmetic once. 61,320 h × 1% × 2.30 × 0.863 × 0.939 = 1,143 t.

**Slide 21.** The decoy. In the original three-week curriculum, teams that sized overtime as a fourth leak lost a point. Say so.

### Size in euros and account strategy (slides 22 to 30)

**Slide 24.** The "Who confirms" column is what turns a guess into a plan. Every assumption has a name next to it.

**Slide 25.** The most common error in gains cases. Make the room say the correct sentence aloud: "€10M of cash once, and €1.8M a year."

**Slide 26.** The triangulation example is real in the data. Both numbers are right on their own perimeter: the annex covers twelve months and all scheduled hours; the logs cover extrusion in Q1. The answer is a question for the controller, not a choice.

**Slides 28 to 30.** Keep this short. The message: Polymex is one site of a group. The first problem must produce a gain Mireille signs, and the pipeline and ontology must be reusable at a sister site. ## Exercise 2, model answer (lab)

The facilitator answer key. Numbers from the Day 1 logs are annualized from Q1 2026 (× 365 / 90) and must be labeled as such.

### Unit economics

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Contribution margin | €447 per tonne (26% of €1,721) |
| One point of extrusion availability | 1,143 good tonnes a year |
| Sellable extra volume | 5,160 t a year (6% of 86,000 t), only if lead times improve, to be confirmed by sales |
| Value cap of all capacity leaks together | 5,160 t × €447 ≈ €2.3M a year |

### Capacity leaks from the logs

| Leak | Physical effect, annualized | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Changeovers | 10.3% of planned time in March; a 40% SMED reduction frees 4.1 points, about 4,700 t a year. At the January rate instead of March, the gap is 272 t a month, about 3,300 t a year | The OTIF story; changeover count rose with retail orders |
| M07 micro-stops | 376 t in Q1, about 1,525 t a year at fleet performance | Unlogged: performance is low while logged downtime is normal |
| M02 downtime | 8.4 points above the fleet on M02: 180 h and 344 t in Q1, about 1,400 t a year | Ask maintenance for the failure log |
| M04 at weekends | 183 good tonnes in Q1 at weekday rates, about 740 t a year | Performance and scrap both worse; Marc's weekday workaround is the hypothesis for Day 4 |
| Shift C | Performance 83.9% against 87.5%: about 310 t of output in Q1 | Overlaps with the machine rows; do not add it |

Total identified capacity: about 8,300 t a year, against 5,160 t sellable. **The capacity leaks cannot be summed at €447.** Their joint value is capped at about €2.3M a year by demand, and only once sales confirms the volume. Below the cap, they are worth avoided overtime. Strong teams write this sentence.

### Leaks from the P&L and annex

| Leak | Size | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Scrap | 4.2% × €89.2M ≈ €3.75M of material, plus €0.15M disposal: **€3.9M a year** of leak. Addressable base case 30%: about €1.2M a year (range 20 to 40%) | Recurring cost-out. Its capacity effect is already inside the 94% quality rate: do not count it again |
| Inventory | (78 − 55) / 365 × COGS €123.8M ≈ **€7.8M of cash, once**, plus 18% carrying ≈ **€1.4M a year** | One-time cash and recurring carry, stated separately |
| Overtime premium | €0.9M a year | Symptom of changeovers and planning. Count only the share not already bought back by the capacity rows; the base case counts zero |

### The ranking we expect

1. **Scrap: €3.9M a year of leak**, of which about €1.2M addressable in the base case. Cost-out, the easiest gain to validate.
2. **Changeovers and machine losses: up to €2.3M a year**, capped by the sellable 6% and conditional on sales confirmation. It is also the OTIF fix.
3. **Inventory: €7.8M of cash once and €1.4M a year of carrying cost.**

Debrief punchline: "€7 to 8M of recurring value sits inside a plant that earns €9.7M of EBIT. This is why the job exists."

### Scoring grid for the debrief (no marks this week, use it to give feedback)

| Dimension | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| Arithmetic | Shown, with an assumptions table and sources |
| The demand fork | Applied explicitly; capacity capped by the 5,160 t |
| Double counts | Overtime named as a symptom; shift C not added to machine rows; scrap capacity not counted twice |
| Memo | Answer first; three leaks; one question each; a director would read it |

### Common failures to name in the debrief

- Valuing freed tonnes at €1,721 (price) or €1,608 (full cost).
- Summing all capacity leaks at €447 per tonne: €3.7M that the market cannot absorb.
- "€9.2M of inventory savings": cash and carry added together.
- Annualizing a quarter without saying so, or annualizing March as if it were the year.
